Research

A practice-led HCI research agenda around AI in everyday contexts.

My research interests focus on Human-AI Interaction, AI-supported learning, independent work systems, reflective wellbeing technologies, and design methods for AI-native products.

Core interests

Research questions I want to investigate.

The goal is not to treat AI as a feature layer, but to understand how people interpret, trust, resist, appropriate, and collaborate with AI systems in real activities.

01

Human-AI interaction for situated learning

How can AI adapt language learning to a user's goals, scenes, routines, and emotional barriers instead of producing generic exercises? SoloSpeak is a product probe for studying scenario-based learning, practice motivation, and AI-mediated feedback.

02

AI-supported independent work

How do solo creators coordinate strategy, execution, finance, and reflection when AI tools change the relationship between individual and organization?

03

Reflective wellbeing and care technologies

How can digital systems support reassurance, self-awareness, and care without becoming intrusive, clinical, or over-quantified?

04

Design methods for AI-native products

How should designers prototype, evaluate, and communicate AI product experiences when behavior is probabilistic, contextual, and continuously evolving?

05

Trust, explainability, and ambiguity

How should AI systems persuade, explain, or withhold certainty? When should collaboration be explicit, and when can ambiguity make interaction more flexible?

Additional research thread

Computational perception and adversarial AI.

Computational AI

Scene-adaptive multi-detector adversarial camouflage

A research project on adversarial camouflage against camouflaged object detectors. The work explores how scene-aware visual patterns and multi-detector optimization can affect model perception while maintaining visual naturalness.

How it fits

Why include it in an HCI portfolio?

It can be framed as technical background in AI perception, model behavior, and system robustness. For HCI applications, it should not dominate the narrative; it should support your broader interest in how AI systems perceive, interpret, and become trusted by humans.
